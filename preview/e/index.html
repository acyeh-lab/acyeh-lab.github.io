The donor immune system meets a trillion microbial strangers.

Allogeneic stem cell transplantation transfers a naïve immune system into a body already colonized by a vast microbial community. We work out how T cells read that community — which antigens they see, which clones expand, and why those choices decide between graft-versus-host disease and durable immunity.

Research

01

Microbial antigens and T cell clonal selection

Graft-versus-host disease has long been explained by genetic disparity between donor and host. We showed that the microbiota independently shapes which T cell clones expand after transplant — meaning some of the immune conflict is directed at microbes, not the patient.

02

Microbial immunopeptidomics

If T cells respond to bacterial antigens, which ones? We combine mass spectrometry, MHC binding prediction and bacterial genomics to identify the specific peptides presented after transplant, and to test whether they drive disease.

03

Spatial biology of the gut interface

Host and microbe meet across a few hundred microns of intestinal tissue. Using spatial transcriptomics with custom bacterial probes, we map immune cells and the microbes they encounter in the same section of tissue.

04

Engineering better cellular therapy

What we learn about antigen recognition feeds back into treatment — targeted T cell therapies, smarter conditioning, and strategies that separate graft-versus-tumor benefit from graft-versus-host toxicity.
