How does a transplanted immune system learn to read the microbiome?

Allogeneic stem cell transplantation transfers a naïve immune system into a body already colonized by a vast microbial community. We work out how T cells read that community — which antigens they see, which clones expand, and why those choices decide between graft-versus-host disease and durable immunity.

We build our own tools where none exist: bacterial pan-genome probe design, microbial MHC binding prediction, and spatial pipelines that put host and microbe in the same image. Code is open on GitHub.

Research directions

Microbial antigens & T cell clonal selection

The microbiota independently shapes which T cell clones expand after transplant — some of the immune conflict is directed at microbes, not the patient.

Microbial immunopeptidomics

Mass spectrometry, MHC binding prediction and bacterial genomics to identify the specific peptides presented after transplant.

Spatial biology of the gut interface

Spatial transcriptomics with custom bacterial probes, mapping immune cells and the microbes they encounter in the same tissue section.

Engineering better cellular therapy

Separating graft-versus-tumor benefit from graft-versus-host toxicity using what we learn about antigen recognition.
