Decoding how T cells read the microbiome after transplant.

Allogeneic stem cell transplantation drops a naïve immune system into a body already colonized by trillions of microbes. We work out which antigens those T cells see — and why that decides between graft-versus-host disease and durable immunity.

Our research

Four connected lines of work, from antigen discovery to the clinic.

01

Microbial antigens & clonal selection

GVHD has long been explained by donor–host genetic disparity. We showed the microbiota independently shapes which T cell clones expand — some of the conflict is aimed at microbes, not the patient.

02

Microbial immunopeptidomics

If T cells respond to bacterial antigens, which ones? Mass spectrometry, MHC binding prediction and bacterial genomics to name the peptides presented after transplant.

03

Spatial biology of the gut

Host and microbe meet across a few hundred microns of intestine. Spatial transcriptomics with custom bacterial probes maps immune cells and the microbes they meet in one section.

04

Better cellular therapy

What we learn about antigen recognition feeds back into treatment — separating graft-versus-tumor benefit from graft-versus-host toxicity.
